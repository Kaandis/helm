# Changelog

## 1.0.3 — 2026-11-05

- Copy, Move to…, Copy to… and Duplicate: the everyday file moves, right on the ⌃ wheel (Copy to… on the big helm). Move to… and Copy to… remember the last folder.
- A lighter start: the big helm begins with the everyday tools (a couple of conversions, the most useful tools, copy and move) and shows their names when it has room. Start Over with Everyday Tools in Settings › Big Helm brings that back any time.
- The ⌃ wheel is shorter: Mail and Terminal moved to the big helm’s extras.

## 1.0.2 — 2026-11-01

- Arrange the big helm by hand: in Settings › Big Helm (or from Harbor, or the menu bar’s Arrange the Big Helm…), drag tool tiles onto a live helm in your shape and look, drag them round it to reorder, or off it to remove.
- The big helm keeps your order, and shades each run of one wheel’s tools.

## 1.0.1 — 2026-11-01

- Picking the big helm's tools now works from Harbor too: each tool is its own switch, shown once "Show every tool that fits" is off. A pick made in Helm 1.0 carries over.

## 1.0 — 2026-11-01

- First release. Drag files and hold ⇧, ⌘ or ⌃ to open the Convert, Tools or Actions wheel under the pointer; drop on a slice and the result lands next to the original.
- Shake a drag side to side and a big helm opens with every tool that fits; hold a wheel key to narrow it down.
- Cut: files wait on a shelf until you drag it into a folder or press ⌥⌘V there. Plus Remove Background, Copy Text (photos and PDFs), Copy Image and Mail.
- Convert photos, PDF pages, videos and audio; compress, halve, clean, merge, split and mute; extract and zip; AirDrop, copy paths and open Terminal. All with ImageIO, PDFKit and AVFoundation, offline.
- No permissions. Stays out of full-screen apps and apps you exclude, and can be paused from the menu bar.
- A slim helm in Liquid Glass or cream; colour, transparency, size, round or square, and handles are all settings. So are hold-or-press keys, which tools the big helm shows, and how firm a shake must be.
- Settings in Helm or in Harbor; English and Turkish.
